In simple words

This demonstration shows five green results, two amber results, and one red result. Colors help organize information, but only a qualified professional can interpret a real medical report in context.

Normal examples

Total cholesterol: 170 mg/dL

Cholesterol is one part of heart-health assessment. Clinicians also look at other cholesterol values and individual risk factors.

Ask your doctor: Should I review my full cholesterol profile?

Creatinine: 0.9 mg/dL

Creatinine is commonly used when assessing kidney function. A clinician considers it alongside other tests and personal factors.

Ask your doctor: Does my kidney function need follow-up?

Sodium: 140 mmol/L

Sodium helps regulate fluid balance and nerve function. Reference ranges can differ by laboratory.

Ask your doctor: Do my electrolyte results need review?

Potassium: 4.1 mmol/L

Potassium is important for normal muscle and heart function. Individual care decisions require a clinical assessment.

Ask your doctor: Is my potassium appropriate for my health needs?

Platelets: 250 ×10⁹/L

Platelets help blood clot. This is an illustrative value, not a reading from a patient's report.

Ask your doctor: Are my blood counts within the expected range?

Borderline examples

Hemoglobin: 11.2 g/dL

Hemoglobin carries oxygen in the blood. The meaning of this sample value depends on age, sex, symptoms, and the laboratory reference range.

Ask your doctor: Is this hemoglobin result relevant to my symptoms?

White blood cells: 11.2 ×10⁹/L

White blood cells are part of the immune system. A health professional considers changes alongside symptoms and other results.

Ask your doctor: Is this blood count finding important?

Urgent label example

This red label follows the wireframe. It is not a real triage decision.

Blood sugar (fasting): 180 mg/dL

The example fasting glucose value is elevated. This red label reproduces the class wireframe; it does not determine how urgently a real patient needs care.

Ask your doctor: Do I need another blood sugar test?
